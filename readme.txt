Nebula Game

An audio space shooter, ported from iOS to Windows.

Nebula Game was made for iPhone by Gray Company, and designed to be played with VoiceOver. You fly a ship through a nebula, listening for enemy ships, their shots and their lasers, and steer to dodge them or line up your own shots. Everything you need is in the sound. This port brings the original game to Windows as faithfully as possible, with the same sounds, voice lines, levels and story.


Getting started

Put on headphones. Enemies are placed left and right in stereo, and the game depends on it.

Run Nebula.exe. The game speaks through your screen reader (NVDA, JAWS and others are supported) and has its own voice for in-game announcements.

On the first launch the game asks whether you'd like to play the tutorial. If you are new, say yes. The tutorial has six short parts and teaches everything below by ear.


The main menu

Campaign: the story, nine chapters of four levels each.
Free Mode: endless play against the clock, for a high score.
Tutorial: the six lessons.
Systems: the settings, described further down.
Hangar: spend the points you earn in the campaign on upgrades for life, shield, ship speed, attack speed and the power shot.
About: the credits.
Exit: close the game. It is spoken only; the menu picture has no Exit button.


Keyboard

Left and right arrows: steer. Hold Shift as well to steer faster.
Space: fire. Space or Enter also skips a chapter's intro.
Escape: pause during play. In menus, go back.
L: hear how much life you have left.

In menus:
Up and down arrows: move between items.
Home and End: jump to the first and last item.
Enter: choose the item.
Escape: go back.
Control: stop the current announcement.
F1, from the main menu: open Systems.

In the campaign selector:
Left and right arrows: change chapter.
Up and down arrows: move between the chapter, Back and the four levels.
Enter: start the level.


Game controller

Any controller Windows recognises works, including PlayStation and Xbox controllers. The first one connected is the one used. Connecting or disconnecting a controller is announced.

D-pad or left stick, left and right: steer.
D-pad or left stick, up and down: move through menus.
Cross on PlayStation, A on Xbox: fire during play; choose in menus; skip a chapter's intro.
Circle on PlayStation, B on Xbox: go back in menus.
Options on PlayStation, Start on Xbox: pause.
Triangle on PlayStation, Y on Xbox: hear your life.
L1 and R1, or LB and RB: jump to the first and last menu item.

Tilt steering: a controller with a motion sensor, such as a DualSense or DualShock 4, can steer the way the iPhone did. Roll the controller to the right to fly right and to the left to fly left. A small roll drifts slowly and a bigger roll moves faster. Holding a direction on the D-pad, stick or keyboard takes over from the roll while you hold it. Xbox controllers have no motion sensor, so they steer with the D-pad or stick.

Vibration:
A short buzz when you are hit.
A long, strong buzz when your ship explodes.
A small pulse when you collect a power-up.
A buzz in the left grip when you reach the left edge of the screen, and in the right grip at the right edge.


Systems (settings)

Move through the options with the up and down arrows and change the selected one with the left and right arrows. Each option explains itself when you move onto it. Changes are saved at once.

Steering speed: how fast the ship moves while you hold a steering key or button. 0.1 to 1.0, default 0.3.
Fast steering speed, with Shift: the same while holding Shift. Default 0.6.
Controller tilt steering: on or off.
Controller tilt sensitivity: how far you need to roll the controller. Higher means a smaller roll steers harder.
Danger zone width: how wide the area is where enemy shots and lasers sound straight ahead of you. Higher values also centre near misses, so you start dodging sooner.
Controller vibration: on or off.
Sound volume, music volume and speech volume: 0 to 100 percent. Speech means the game's own voice and the story narration, not your screen reader.
Speech rate: how fast the game's own voice talks. 1 is the original speed.
Language: follow Windows, English or Portuguese. Takes effect the next time the game starts.
Window size: the size of the game window, for anyone watching.


Language and voices

The game is in English and Portuguese, and follows your Windows display language unless you choose one in Systems. The game's own voice uses the Windows voices installed for that language. To hear the game in Portuguese with a Portuguese voice, install a Portuguese voice in Windows settings, under Time and language, Speech.


Saved games and settings

Your progress and settings are kept in a Nebula folder, and progress is saved as you play.
On Windows: inside your AppData Roaming folder (type %APPDATA%\Nebula in the Run dialog to open it).
On a Mac: Library, Application Support, Nebula, in your home folder.
On Linux: .local/share/Nebula in your home folder.


Differences from the iPhone version

The port aims to be an exact copy of the original. The differences are:
Keys and controller buttons take the place of tilting the phone, tapping and swiping.
The full campaign is unlocked from the start; on iPhone it was a purchase. Levels still unlock one by one as you beat them.
Multiplayer (versus mode) is not available. It depended on Apple's Game Center.
Leaderboards and analytics are removed.
The Systems screen, controller support, vibration and Home and End jumps are new.
The danger zone makes attack sounds centred across the whole area where they can hit you.
A bug that could leave chapter 7, level 1 unable to finish is fixed.
The About screen's links to Gray Company's website and email are removed, as the company no longer exists.
